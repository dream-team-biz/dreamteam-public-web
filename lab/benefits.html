Benefits

More finished work from every engineer: a team behind each one.

Measured on your own numbers.

Dream Team gives each engineer a team of AI agents; your engineers decide. A pilot counts merged, reviewed and tested changes per engineer against your last 8–12 weeks of history.

1–2 hours of the engineer's own time on one merged task

The core benefits

A team does the work; the decisions, and the reasons, stay yours

The problem: Why Dream Team? → · How it runs: The Dream Team Life Cycle →

  • Each task gets the roles it needs, from architect to tester

    Elaborate

    Architect, developers, reviewer, tester and technical writer; the strongest model designs and reviews, a cheaper one implements.

    On record: T-393 had no design work, so its design steps were skipped: it ran build, review, test and documentation.

  • A line of code leads back to its task and its decision

    Elaborate

    A task's merged commit names the task; its record names its epic and, where there was one, the brainstorm behind it. Lesson 4 on Why Dream Team? says why that matters.

    On record: the code's own note names T-687 and the engineer's decision at its gate.

  • Agents find design mistakes in the contract before any code

    Elaborate

    Up to three agents, four for a structural change, read the contract before the engineer's go-ahead; the reviewer is always among them.

    On record: on T-707, that review led to twelve corrections to the task before any code was written.

  • A person decides on every task.

    Elaborate

    Your own branch protection and required reviews apply, as you set them. The agents find and propose; the engineer chooses at the gates.

    On record: on T-707, the engineer's sign-off rested on the run report, the review findings and the demo replay, not the diff.

Heads of engineering

Delivery you can plan; teams on Claude Code or GitHub Copilot keep it

You answer for what ships and what it costs.

  • A goal becomes a plan: the tasks, their order, and which would collide.Elaborate

    Each link in a task's dependency record says why one waits on the other.

    On record: the plan of one goal on Dream Team's own board; the record card shows it.

  • Your senior engineers spend their time on the plan and the result.Elaborate

    The agents build, review and test; your engineers keep what only a person can judge: what to build, and whether it is done.

    On record: on T-707, the engineer sent six messages: four that started its stages, and two in the first session, one of them the decision at the gate.

  • Runs on Claude Code and GitHub Copilot, from one process definition.Elaborate

    If your teams use Claude Code or GitHub Copilot, they keep it. Both setups come from one source, and CI fails a build whose agent files drift from it.

    On record: on 26 June 2026, T-276 was the first Dream Team task run from VS Code Copilot: its orchestrator dispatched the architect, the reviewer and the technical writer.

  • Made for the codebase you already have (brownfield): Dream Team studies it first.Elaborate

    Before it writes code, Dream Team's archaeologist studies your codebase and writes a Discovery Report. Its first pass is a mechanical scan, before any AI review; with your consent it writes its findings and report into Dream Team's own folder in your repository, and nothing more until you ask.

    On record: on Dream Team's own repository, about 630,000 lines of code, the first, mechanical pass mapped its layout, where the code changes most and what depends on what.

Team leads

Work arrives checked, on its own branch, and reports its own status

You keep a team's work moving.

  • Your reviewers get the findings, the fixes and the test results with the change.Elaborate

    The agents' review and the tests run before a person looks. Lesson 2 on Why Dream Team? says why that matters now.

    On record: T-707's code review and test run; the record card shows both.

  • Each task runs on its own branch and, on Claude Code, in its own working copy.Elaborate

    Each change then lands through GitHub's merge queue, which runs the required checks on it before it merges. On Copilot a task runs in the main checkout.

    On record: T-707 ran on its own branch, in its own working copy.

  • Status without the status meeting: one report shows what moved and what is stuck.Elaborate

    The report is made on demand, from the board and the runlogs. Jira gets comments and status changes automatically.

    On record: T-687 built it: what changed, what is newly unblocked, what is stuck, and the next best action.

  • Test debt is planned as tasks, and documentation is a step of the run.Elaborate

    Both are on the board and in the record, not left for later.

    On record: the Tech Debt epic had 26 of 26 tasks closed by 9 August 2026; on T-707 the architect recorded the decision, the technical writer the changelog and history.

Developers

The reasons, the review and the tests come with the code

You want code you would put your name to.

  • The task's record shows how it was reasoned: what was found, and what was decided.Elaborate

    That is the mechanism. Whether it makes engineers better is not measured, and we don't claim it.

    On record: on T-707, the backend developer and, separately, the architect found a hazard to loading the board; the architect proposed the fix.

  • A reviewer agent with no editing tools, and a tester, check every code change.Elaborate

    By default the reviewer runs on the strongest model and the developer on a cheaper one; on T-707 both ran on the strongest. If two review rounds don't settle it, the task stops for the engineer. The tester maps each criterion to a test, and a failing test the change caused stops the task.

    On record: T-707's code review and tests; the record card shows what they found.

  • The agents are technology-neutral: your team's own skills rank first, and your conventions are wired in where you keep them.Elaborate

    Know-how for your stack comes as skills. For each one your team needs, your own wins if you have it; next comes Dream Team's reviewed library; a skill drafted for your stack needs a person's sign-off. Your conventions are referenced where you keep them, so they stay yours.

    On record: T-707's contract named the nine skills its agents were to load, from code review to Python testing.

  • Context lives in the repository as long-term project memory, so a new session picks up where work stopped.Elaborate

    If a run stops, the orchestrator reads the runlog, finds the steps already done and resumes at the first incomplete one.

    On record: T-707's run; the record card shows its sessions.

Business leaders

You pay for results you can count and costs you can see

You approve the budget and answer for it.

  • You judge the return on your own numbers, against your team's own history.Elaborate

    The pilot counts changes per engineer and keeps defects and rework beside them, on the same baseline. A pilot of weeks cannot show how the code holds up over years.

    On record: our own month, rework counted: 248 work items closed in 32 days, 9 July – 9 August 2026, 49 of the 231 tasks repairs. Volume, not a return.

  • You see what each task cost, and what drives the bill.Elaborate

    Every step records the tokens and time it actually used; totals are checked for consistency. Not yet reconciled with provider invoices.

    On record: T-707's cost; the record card shows it.

  • What was left for later stays with the product.Elaborate

    Follow-ups go into the task's closure notes, where a later task can take one up.

    On record: T-707's follow-ups; the record card shows them.

  • Our engineers set the cycle up with your team: Your Dream Team in 8 weeks.Elaborate

    The program, in three steps: set up the cycle in your repositories; ship your first features through it, together; teach your team to run it.

Security

No server of ours; deletes, force-pushes and POSTs ask first

You answer for what runs and what leaves.

  • No Dream Team server: the whole history lives in your Git.Elaborate

    What leaves goes to the model provider (your code and the context a task needs) and to the services you connect: GitHub, Jira, the web pages the agents read. None of it goes to us.

    On record: T-707's merged commit carried its task records with its code, 39 files in all.

  • Deleting, force-pushing, rewriting history and outbound posts wait for a person.Elaborate

    The permission profile Dream Team installs asks before them, refuses to read .env files, and pre-approves commits, pushes, Python and web reads, which run without asking. Dream Team's own rules call its hooks “process discipline — NOT a security control”: the control is your branch protection and merge queue.

    On record: the profile ships with Dream Team's installer, in .claude/settings.json.

  • Changes made by AI carry a paper trail: the task's contract and its runlog.Elaborate

    Audit and incident questions start from a task, not from someone's memory.

    On record: 96% of Dream Team's closed tasks have both.

  • Our engineers install it with you. The installer lists every file before it adds one.Elaborate

    The installer writes nothing you have not seen and approved. Your install contract, .dt/contract.yaml, is one file you approve: which files are Dream Team's and which are yours, and when a push must ask first. A hook blocks writes and pushes outside it, but lets them through if it cannot run; until you approve and commit the contract, the agents' writes are not checked against it.

    On record: the installer's file list, and a defect Dream Team found in it and fixed; the record card shows both.

  • The first scan of your code reads your assistant's settings and runs nothing.Elaborate

    The task that built its security screen checked that it executes nothing.

    On record: on Dream Team's own repository, it flagged our own unscoped auto-approve shell permissions.
